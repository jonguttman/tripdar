# Tripdar Product Photo — Photographer's Checklist

*Psilly by Nature, Enchanted by Choice.*

A one-page guide for shooting products on your iPhone so they pass **catalog-safe on the first try**. The pipeline can straighten, clean, and light an image — but it will never rewrite a label. If the text isn't readable in your shot, the product goes to the retake pile. Everything below is aimed at one thing: **a sharp, honest, fully-in-frame label.**

Print this. Tape it near where you shoot.

---

## The 60-second glance

- [ ] Wipe the container — no dust, no fingerprints, no lint
- [ ] Plain, uncluttered background; one product in frame
- [ ] Phone on a tripod, level, lens centered on the **label**
- [ ] Camera slightly **above the middle** of the container, looking straight on
- [ ] Step back and use **optical zoom** — never wide-angle up close, never digital zoom
- [ ] Whole product in frame: full cap, full base, breathing room on all sides
- [ ] Tap to focus, then **lock focus + exposure**
- [ ] **Portrait / depth mode OFF.** Flash off. Highest resolution on
- [ ] Shoot **front**, then **rear**, then optional **side** — as separate photos

---

## Before you shoot

**Clean the product first.** Dust, lint, and fingerprints are the number-one cause of retakes. The pipeline removes tiny stray specks, but it will not touch anything near printed text — so a smudge across the label means a retake. Wipe the container and cap with a soft cloth and check it under the light before the first frame.

**Clear the background.** A plain, matte, uncluttered surface. No other products, no props, no busy patterns. One product per photo — two items in the frame confuses the crop and the pipeline will reject it.

**Set your light.** Soft, even light. To kill glare, move the light source **higher and farther to the side** rather than pointing it straight at the container. A glare hotspot over the label can't be repaired without touching the text, so it's an automatic retake.

---

## Framing

**Center the lens on the label**, not on the top of the cap. The label is the part that has to be perfect.

**Shoot from slightly above the middle** of the container, aimed straight on. Too low and you're shooting up into the cap; too high and you lose the base. Just above center, looking level, keeps the whole front readable.

**Keep the entire product in frame** — the full cap at the top, the full base at the bottom, and a comfortable margin of empty space all the way around. That whitespace gives the pipeline room to straighten and re-center without clipping anything. A cropped cap or base means the pipeline can't confirm the product is whole, and it flags for retake.

**Don't tilt the product to "show it off."** Upright and square to the camera. The pipeline straightens small tilts; it won't rebuild a label shot at a steep angle.

---

## Distance & zoom

**Step back and zoom in with optical zoom.** Getting close with a wide-angle lens bends the edges and fattens the middle of the container — the label warps and the geometry check fails.

**Optical zoom only. Never digital zoom.** Digital zoom throws away resolution and softens the text. On iPhone, stay at the true lens focal lengths (1x, or the 2x/3x/5x telephoto if your model has one) — don't pinch past them into the blurry digital range.

**Fill the frame with the product, but leave the margin.** Product large enough that the text is crisp; small enough that nothing touches the edges.

---

## Camera settings

**Use a tripod.** Handheld shake is the most common cause of soft, unreadable labels. A tripod (or bracing the phone against something solid) fixes it instantly.

**Lock focus and exposure.** Tap the label on screen to focus, then press and hold until it locks (AE/AF LOCK on iPhone). This stops the phone from re-focusing or re-metering between shots and keeps a product family consistent.

**Turn OFF Portrait mode and any depth / background-blur effect.** Artificial blur softens the edges and confuses background removal. We need a real, sharp photo — the studio look is added later.

**Shoot at the highest available resolution**, and turn the flash off. Bright, even ambient light beats a hard on-camera flash every time. (Minimum usable resolution is 1200 px on the long edge; more headroom is always better.)

---

## Per product — three views

Shoot each view as its own photo, in order:

1. **Front** — the primary label. This is the hero and the one that must be flawless.
2. **Rear** — ingredients, dosage, warnings, and regulatory text.
3. **Side** *(optional)* — only if the side carries information worth capturing.

Same distance, same zoom, same height for every view of the same product, so the family looks like a set.

---

## Why this matters

The pipeline is built to protect the truth of the product. It will happily clean the surface, drop out the background, warm up the whites, and ground the product with a soft shadow. What it will **never** do is guess at a letter, a number, a dosage, or a logo. That's the promise that keeps the catalog honest — and it only works if the label arrives sharp and whole. Give it a clean, level, well-lit frame and it does the rest.
